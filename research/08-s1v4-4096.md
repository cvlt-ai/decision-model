# s1-v4 (4096-context retrain) — confirmed, but v3 stays our best

`checkpoints/s1-v4`: same order-augmented + boolq mixture_v3 (131,698 rows), 1
epoch, **trained at `--max-len 4096`** (was 1024), bs=1/accum=32 (effective batch
32, 4,021 steps). Trained clean (loss 6.30 → 0.267, min 0.109). The SIGBUS guards
(expandable_segments + OMP/MKL=8) held — attempt 2 completed with no crash.

## Why we trained v4
v3's JevBench hard tier (long-policy documents) was our biggest gap vs
AlexWortega. A 4096 *re-score* of v3 recovered only 4/41 truncated hard items, so
the hypothesis was: the model was never *trained* on long documents, so retrain at
4096. **Pre-flight caveat that turned out decisive:** the mixture has only **40
rows over 1024 tokens** (banking77 maxes at 792) — so a 4096 retrain makes the
model *capable* of long context but the data barely *exercises* it.

## Result: the hypothesis is confirmed, and the conclusion is "it's not context"

### JevBench public-231 (the reason v4 exists)
| | v3@1024 | v3@4096 (window) | **v4@4096 (retrained)** | AlexW v5 | Jev 1.13 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| standard (72) | 66 | 66 | 66 | 71 | 71 |
| easy (48) | 48 | 48 | 48 | 48 | 48 |
| **hard (111)** | 42 | **46** | 41 | 69 | 81 |
| **all-public** | 0.675 | **0.693** | 0.671 | 0.814 | 0.866 |

- **v4@1024 = 156/231, identical to v3@1024** → no regression from the retrain.
- **v4@4096 (41 hard) is *below* v3@4096 (46 hard)** → the 13-hour retrain did
  **not** beat the free 4096 *window* on v3. Widening the context and retraining on
  a mostly-short mixture added nothing the window didn't already give.

### Calibration (the one place v4 got worse)
| | ECE@T=1 | best T | ECE@best | Brier@best |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| v3 | 0.062 | 1.3 | **0.0331** | 0.1400 |
| v4 | 0.110 | 1.5 | 0.0433 | **0.1377** |

v4's raw probabilities are *more* miscalibrated (ECE 0.110 vs 0.062) — the
bs=1/4096 training dynamics shifted the confidence scale — so it needs more
scaling (T=1.5) and lands at a slightly worse post-cal ECE (0.0433 vs 0.0331),
though its Brier is marginally best.

## Verdict
**v3 remains our best checkpoint.** v4's value was *diagnostic*, not a win:
1. **No regression** (v4@1024 == v3@1024; frozen macro flat) → the 4096 retrain
   is safe, not harmful.
2. **Confirmed context-capability is not the binding constraint** on the hard tier.
   The retrain didn't beat the free 4096 window. The hard gap is **long-policy
   *data* + general *knowledge***, which the short mixture doesn't supply.

**Best numbers we hold:** v3 frozen (macro 0.727, negation 0.030, post-cal ECE
0.0331) + v3@4096-window for JevBench (0.693, hard 46) — a *free* serving change
that's our best JevBench score.

## What this means for the plan
The next move is **not** more context or another retrain on the same data. It's
**data**: the TSI-breadth mixture (5.3M-row general-instruction set, license-filtered)
to add (a) long-policy documents that exercise the 4096 context v4 now has, and
(b) general knowledge/reasoning that lifts the mmlu_pro (0.25–0.30) and the
"fits-but-wrong" hard items toward AlexWortega's 0.622. Concretely: **mixture_v5
= mixture_v3 + TSI long/general rows**, train at 4096 (v4 proved that's the
context that won't truncate the long TSI rows).
