## Why the VitaminC flip probe "failed" but the model improved
The short (max-929-token) VitaminC flip probe went the WRONG way (NEI acc 0.308→0.264,
lazy 0.175→0.235, overall 0.783→0.774). Yet the long-policy hard tier jumped 46→59.
Not a contradiction: the c2d training pairs are **long policy + state, one decisive
fact** — structurally matching JevBench's hard tier (buried-fact policy docs), NOT the
short VitaminC probe. The c2d line moved the axis it was designed for (long-policy
evidence-sensitivity), which the flip probe was the right instrument to *check* but
honestly reported wasn't where the win landed.

**Lesson:** a 3.3% contrastive line is enough to bend a 4B model on its *in-distribution
structure* (long policy docs) but not on a different, short, human-NLI probe. To move
the short-probe axis specifically, upsample c2d or add short contrastive data.
